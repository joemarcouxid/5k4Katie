The golden rule: jog slow enough to talk in full sentences. If a week feels hard, repeat it — that's not falling behind, that's how it's supposed to work.

Home strength 💪

Twice a week, on days you don't run. About 25 minutes with a pair of dumbbells, a wall and a step. Strong hips, legs and trunk protect the low back and knees and make running feel easier.
How to do it:
  • Move slowly and with control: about 2 seconds down, 2 seconds up. Breathe out on the effort, and never hold your breath.
  • Rest 45–60 seconds between sets.
  • Finish each set feeling you could have done 2–3 more. You should never be straining.
  • When the reps feel easy for two sessions in a row, add 2 reps or a set. Move to the next phase when the app says so and it feels right.
  • Keep a "neutral" back: not rounded, not over-arched. Bend at the hips, not the low back.

Back care & mobility 🧘‍♀️

A guided 9-minute routine. Do it after every walk/jog, and on any day the back feels stiff. The app talks you through each move.
Back-friendly habits
  • A short walk often eases a stiff back better than resting. Gentle movement is good medicine.
  • For the first few weeks, choose flat routes on soft-ish surfaces such as a track, a park path or packed gravel.
  • Jog tall, with short, quick, quiet steps. Don't reach out in front with the foot.
  • Hills: walk the uphills for now.
  • Avoid long sitting right after a workout. Stretch first, or keep moving.

Any days are fine. Just never jog two days in a row.
Why it's built this way
  • Short jogs first. Coming back to running, the heart and lungs adapt fast, but tendons, joints and the back take longer. Walk breaks let them catch up.
  • Jogging time grows a little at a time, and weeks 5 and 8 are lighter "recovery" weeks (marked in green) to absorb the work.
  • Strength days build the hips, thighs, calves and trunk. That's where most running aches, low back included, are prevented.
  • The 5K is the finish line, not a race. Jogging it all the way is the goal, and walk breaks are still a win.
The pain check (0–10 scale)
  • 0–3: normal muscle soreness or mild stiffness. OK to carry on, as long as it has settled by the next morning.
  • 4–5, or worse the next morning: back off. Swap the next jog for a brisk walk, or repeat the previous week.
  • Sharp pain, limping, or pain that changes how you move: stop and rest. If it hasn't eased within a week, get it checked.
Check with a doctor first if there are heart, blood-pressure, chest-pain or dizziness concerns, or any ongoing joint issues.

See someone promptly if back pain comes with numbness or tingling down a leg, leg weakness, or any change in bladder or bowel control. A physical therapist is a great ally if the back keeps flaring.
At the track 🏟️
  • 1 lap = 400 m. 5K = 12½ laps. A mile is about 4 laps.
  • The track is ideal for this plan: flat, soft and predictable, which is easy on the back.
  • Run counter-clockwise. Inside lanes are usually for faster runners, so jog in a middle lane and walk in the outer lanes.
  • Switch direction now and then (if the track allows it) so the same hip and knee aren't always on the inside of the curve.
  • The track is a perfect place for your 5K day. Count 12½ laps, or let the app run and keep going until you've done the laps.
Other tips
  • Get fitted for running shoes at a running store. It's the one thing worth spending on.
  • Sleep and a protein-rich meal after workouts help recovery a lot at any age, and especially after 40.
  • Drink water before and after. Not much is needed during a workout this length.
  • Turn the phone volume up. The app beeps, vibrates and talks at every change, so the phone can stay in a pocket.
